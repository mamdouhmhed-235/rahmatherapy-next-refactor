1 · Getting in

One address, one password.

Bookmark this

Your admin lives at rahmatherapy.uk/admin/login. There is no link to it from your normal website — type it in once and save it. It's a staff door only; clients never sign in.

Why your own numbers say zero

My contribution at the top counts only visits assigned to you. If you don't do visits yourself it will read 0 for ever, and that is correct.

The row underneath — 3 bookings · 4 attention · £160 outstanding · 6 clients — is the whole clinic. That's the one you want. Keep the Team / Mine switch on Team.

If everything reads zero just after you sign in, reload the page. It's the page loading, not lost data.

Finding your way around

The bar across the bottom — Dashboard, Bookings, Clients, Enquiries, Staff, More — never goes away.

The admin is dark by default. If you'd rather it were light, use the Dark dropdown at the top of any screen. It only changes it for you, not for your staff.

2 · Your day, start to finish

Every booking travels the same path. Once you know it, the whole system makes sense.

Booking arrives→ You confirm it→ You give it to a therapist→ The visit happens→ Mark it done & paid
The step people forget

Giving the job to a therapist. The client never sees whether it's been done — they think it's all arranged. A confirmed visit with nobody assigned is the one that hurts.

Your morning

Use the Today tab

The main bookings list starts with the furthest-away date, so today sits partway down. The Today tab is the one you want each morning.

Your evening

Back to the dashboard. Outstanding tells you who still owes money. Enquiries tells you who you promised to ring back. Needs your attention should be empty — if it isn't, that's tomorrow's list.

3 · Confirming a booking

A booking from your website arrives as pending. Confirming tells the client it's really happening.

You can confirm without opening anything: pending cards in the bookings list have a Confirm button on them. Open the booking when you want to do more than that.

Use the buttons, or the boxes — not both

The four buttons act the moment you tap them. The dropdowns below them do nothing until you press Save status & payment.

Doing both is how people end up recording the same payment twice.

The two little buttons on each booking card

The pin opens the address in Maps. The … gives you Send reminder, Mark paid and Cancel booking — the quickest way to nudge someone about tomorrow. On a narrow phone that menu opens half off the edge of the screen. It still works; it just looks wrong.

4 · Giving the job to a therapist

The step that matters most, and the easiest one to miss.

  1. Open the bookingThe "Assign" bar on your dashboard takes you to a list of the ones that need somebody — but you still have to open each booking.
  2. Scroll right to the bottomThe page is long. Keep going past guidance, dates, payment and notes until you reach Participants, which says whether they need a male or female therapist. Assignment is just below it.
  3. Press "Assign therapist"A list slides up from the bottom.
  4. Tap a nameThat's the whole thing. It saves the moment you tap.
It happens the instant you tap

There is no "are you sure?", and emails go straight out to both the client and the therapist. Get the right person first time — changing your mind emails them both again.

The box then stays open and changes to "Reassign this booking". That means it worked — don't tap again.

When nobody can be picked

The most common cause

"Bookings disabled." Every new staff member starts switched off. Until you turn on Can take bookings on their profile, they can't be given any work.

Therapists can also pick work up themselves. Bookings → Claimable shows each person the unassigned visits they're allowed to take, with a Claim button. Assigning and claiming end in the same place.

5 · Finishing a visit

Two taps, and skipping them is the commonest way your numbers go wrong.

  1. Mark completeThe visit happened.
  2. Mark paidIf money changed hands. Cash or card.
If you skip it

The visit still shows as owing money, and the client still counts as someone who's never been. Your end-of-day numbers quietly stop being true.

6 · Moving, cancelling, no-shows

Home visits change. Here's each one.

  • They want to move it. Open the booking → Move appointment. Pick a new day and time. The service, the people, the price and the therapist all stay as they are, and the client is emailed the new time.
  • They cancel. Open the booking → Status & payment → Cancel booking.
  • Nobody answered the door. Don't mark it complete — press Mark no-show in the gold panel at the top of the booking, so your numbers stay honest.

7 · Taking a booking over the phone

Someone rings up. Bookings → New booking, then four steps.

Two things that will stop you dead

You must tap one of the suggested addresses. Typing it out and carrying on leaves the form stuck.

The calendar doesn't appear until postcode, town, client gender and service are all filled in. It looks broken. It's waiting.

Before you press submit

The email is already ticked

"Send confirmation email to client" is ticked when you get there. If you're just typing up a booking you already agreed on the phone, untick it — or you'll email a real person out of the blue.

The consent box next to it is not ticked, and the form won't submit until it is.

"No therapists available on this date"

It doesn't mean you're closed. It means nobody who matches — right gender, switched on, working that day — is free. Override this date books it anyway and leaves it for someone to pick up.

Repeat bookings

Greyed out here. To set up a repeating visit, start from the client's own page instead.

8 · Enquiries

Someone asking a question, not booking yet. This is your "ring them back" list.

  • Mark contacted once you've rung them back.
  • Convert turns the enquiry straight into a booking — no retyping.
  • Record new enquiry logs a phone call that's just come in.

Leaving enquiries sitting on New is how you lose work. The Conversion this month figure at the top is the score for exactly that.

9 · Clients

Everyone who's ever booked, and their history.

Two things on this page

Delete sits right next to Edit at the top of a client's record. Mind your thumb.

The tick-boxes down the left of the list select several clients at once, and they're easy to catch while scrolling. If a bar appears at the bottom of the screen, you've ticked something — untick it.

Two things to ignore

The thin diagonal line under the statistics is a trend chart with only one visit to draw. Nothing is broken.

The Returning / New labels don't always agree with each other on a client with one or two visits. Trust the booking history at the bottom — that's the real record.

10 · Adding someone to your team

Staff → Add staff member. This creates their account and their password in one go.

Two things, or it won't work

The password must be at least 12 characters, or it won't be accepted.

Their gender isn't paperwork — it decides which clients they can ever be sent to. Many clients ask for a same-gender therapist.

Then do this, or they can't work

New staff start with bookings switched off. Open their profile and turn on Can take bookings, or they'll never appear when you try to assign a visit.

This is the single thing people miss most. On the staff list they'll show as "Bookings off" and "Onboarding 5/6" until you do it.

The switches save instantly

Active account and Can take bookings take effect the moment you tap them. The Save profile button further up is only for their name and details.

When someone leaves

Don't delete them — set their role to Inactive / Suspended. That switches off their access and keeps their history.

11 · When someone forgets their password

This one is entirely your job, and it has a time limit.

  1. They tap "Forgot your password?"Nothing is emailed to them yet. The request comes to you.
  2. You go to More → Password requestsEvery waiting request is listed there.
  3. You press ApproveThey're emailed a one-time link — and the same link is shown on your screen, so you can read it out or message it to them.
  4. They set a new passwordUsing that link. At least 12 characters.
Approve it the same day

Requests and links both last 24 hours. After that they have to start again.

If YOU forget your own password

There's nobody above you to approve it. Keep your own sign-in details written down somewhere safe, and if you're ever locked out, contact whoever looks after the website for you.

12 · The four roles

Each role sees a different home screen. A therapist's app really is smaller — that's on purpose. Give people the smallest role that lets them do their job.

Owner

Give it to: yourself. One person.

  • Everything, including changing what other people are allowed to do.
  • The only role that can change permissions.

Admin / Practice Manager

Give it to: a trusted manager who runs things while you're treating.

  • Everything you can do — bookings, clients, staff, prices, opening hours, money.
  • Their dashboard is identical to yours.
  • Can't change permissions.
"Admin" is not a limited role

The name makes it sound safe. It isn't — an Admin can do almost everything you can, including changing prices and adding staff. Only hand it to someone you'd trust with the business.

Client Care / Booking Coordinator

Give it to: whoever answers the phone and the messages.

  • Enquiries, all bookings, all clients — confirm, move and cancel.
  • Can give a booking to a therapist, which is the job worth handing over.
  • Can't change prices, services, opening hours or settings.
  • Can't add or edit staff — they see a read-only team list.
  • Can't claim a visit for themselves. Claiming is for people who do visits.
About money

Takings are kept off their dashboard — you'll see no revenue and no outstanding figure. Treat that as tidiness rather than a lock, and give this role to someone you'd trust with the numbers anyway.

Therapist

Give it to: everyone who does visits.

  • Their own day, their own visits, and the address and phone number for each.
  • Mark a visit complete, and add treatment notes.
  • Claim open work that matches them, and set their own availability.
  • Can't see the client list, or anyone else's bookings.
  • Can't see the clinic's takings.
A fifth choice

Inactive / Suspended isn't really a role — it's how you switch somebody off without deleting them.

13 · Services and hours

The two settings you'll actually touch. Both live under More.

Check this number first

If Female says 0, every client who asks for a woman will be told there's no availability — no matter how empty your calendar looks. Same for Male. It's the fastest answer to "why can't anyone book?"

Changing a price

"In use" means real bookings are attached to that service. Changing the price doesn't rewrite bookings you've already taken. If you stop offering something, retire it rather than deleting it.

14 · Quick reminders

The short version, for when something surprises you.

  • A new person can't be given work until you turn on Can take bookings on their profile.
  • Assigning happens the instant you tap a name, and emails both the client and the therapist. The box re-titling itself "Reassign" means it worked.
  • The "Assign" bar doesn't assign. It takes you to the list; you still open each booking.
  • "Claimable" is personal. Today and Upcoming show the whole clinic; Claimable shows only what the person looking at it could take. Two people see different numbers.
  • "Next visit: nothing scheduled" above a busy day means nothing is scheduled later today. Visits already past don't count.
  • If a screen looks empty or the numbers look impossible, reload it before worrying. It's usually the page, not your data.
Nothing gets deleted behind your back

Every change is recorded with who did it and when, and deletions always ask first. Only two things happen instantly with no confirmation: assigning a therapist, and the two switches on a staff profile.
